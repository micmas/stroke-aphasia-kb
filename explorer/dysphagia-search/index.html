1. Prepare your first search

Use your usual Aphasia KB access: PubMed, and UQ Library for Scopus and APA PsycINFO on EBSCOhost. The other sources remain part of this search; confirm their platform and coverage through the library.

Ready for pilot searching; not yet a locked systematic-search protocol. Database syntax guidance has been checked, but these searches have not been run in your subscriptions. For databases with a text-word pilot, add verified subject headings before the formal run. Record pilot decisions separately and recheck them after the protocol is locked.

Two broad concepts

Stroke AND swallowing. No imaging or therapy filter at this stage: those details may be missing from abstracts. Apply the chapter’s evidence criteria when screening.

Consistent limits

Publication year 2000 through your search date. No language, full-text availability, adult or study-design search filters. Assess age, population and publication type during screening. Track earlier pillar papers separately.

Queries with date syntax update below. For other databases, apply the publication-date limit in the interface and record the exact limit. Year-only filters include the full end year; record that difference.

2. Search all eight databases

Open each source through your library where needed. Copy its query, run it, inspect warnings and save all result batches. Counts are deliberately blank until you record an actual search.

3. Start title-and-abstract screening

Combine your exports in your reference manager and review duplicates. Match DOI or PMID first, then title/year; keep every database source and retain the original files. Different analyses from a shared cohort are not automatically duplicate papers.

Retain or mark unsure

Adults after stroke, with swallowing impairment, swallowing recovery or treatment response, and at least one potentially relevant evidence type:

  • Brain imaging or lesion relationships with swallowing outcomes.
  • Patient characteristics predicting or modifying response to a named treatment.
  • A systematic/scoping review or meta-analysis of relevant evidence.
  • A narrative review specifically covering lesion or neuroimaging evidence.

If imaging, subgroup data or design are unclear, retain for full text or mark unsure.

Exclude only when clear

  • No stroke population or no swallowing outcome.
  • A clearly ineligible report: protocol, editorial or conference abstract without a full report.
  • A primary study with neither an imaging–outcome relationship nor patient-factor analysis of treatment response.
  • A clearly out-of-scope age group, date or study design.

Use one reason and a short note. Do not exclude an abstract simply because full text is unavailable, aphasia is absent, or statistical results are null.

These are pilot criteria adapted from the Aphasia KB’s imaging-focused scope. General treatment efficacy and non-imaging natural-recovery studies enter through syntheses initially. Full-text eligibility and any scope amendments must be settled before final inclusion. A screening decision never approves a KB finding.

Make your screening sheet

The converter does not deduplicate or make screening decisions. It preserves one row per input record.

Decision fields and full-text checks

Use include to advance to full text, exclude for a clear exclusion, or unsure for unresolved eligibility. Record your name, date and reason. Suggested reason IDs: not_stroke, not_dysphagia, age_out_of_scope, no_imaging_primary, therapy_no_patient_factors, narrative_review_out_of_scope, out_of_date_range, design_out_of_scope, not_full_report, grey_literature, duplicate_report, other.

At full text, verify adult/stroke-specific subgroups, the actual analysis and outcome, and source extractability. Carry over the existing minimum of five participants for lesion-mapping or therapy claims, with a documented classic-pillar exception. Record language barriers and reports not retrieved separately; unavailable full text is not an exclusion for scientific cause. Keep all decision history when the protocol changes.

Before the formal screening round

  1. Check each translation and controlled vocabulary in the selected platform.
  2. Check retrieval of a small set of known relevant papers; investigate every miss.
  3. Save a dated, versioned final strategy and document changes from this pilot.
  4. Run all eight sources, record unavailable access, and deduplicate with provenance.
  5. Recheck pilot decisions against the final protocol, then continue screening.

A useful candidate benchmark is Mapping acute lesion locations to physiological swallow impairments after stroke. Add further benchmarks spanning recovery, treatment response and synthesis; one retrieved paper is not a recall assessment.
