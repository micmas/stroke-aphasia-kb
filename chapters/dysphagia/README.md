# Dysphagia: protocol preparation

Status: draft. No formal searches or eligibility decisions have been made under
this chapter protocol. Do not import dysphagia records into the existing
aphasia-only screening portal until chapter-specific writing and coverage are
implemented.

## Proposed scope

Adults following stroke; extract a stroke-specific subgroup where mixed
populations permit it. Questions cover lesion/network associations with swallowing
impairments, recovery predictors, treatment response and relevant syntheses.
Co-occurring aphasia is a characteristic, not an inclusion requirement.

Initially retain the existing imaging-focused primary-study scope and its
patient-factor/treatment-response exception, 2000-onward search window and
citation-chased pillar exception. These are proposed defaults to lock in a
versioned protocol before formal searching, not completed screening decisions.
General efficacy and instrument-validation evidence need explicit eligibility
rules. Preserve separate decisions for each chapter when a paper covers both.

references and forward citations as separately recorded identification sources.
Trial registries require an explicit amendment if ongoing/unpublished studies
are included.

## Extraction pilot

Use 8–12 varied main papers spanning lesion mapping, swallowing physiology,
recovery, treatment response and synthesis. A dysphagia-experienced reviewer
creates source-linked reference annotations. Compare the current extraction
configuration with a versioned adaptation, then evaluate additional held-out
papers before scaling.

Adapt both source-context extraction and relationship mapping. Preserve assessed
subgroups, dysphagia definition, co-occurring aphasia, VFSS/FEES or bedside method,
exact instrument/version, score polarity, thresholds, bolus conditions, timing,
intervention/comparator/dose and relevant adjustments. Keep physiology,
penetration/aspiration, oral intake, tube dependence and complications distinct.
Missing source information remains missing; do not infer anatomy or score meaning.
Current model reconciliation does not approve findings. Existing source review,
visual verification and human approval obligations remain applicable.
