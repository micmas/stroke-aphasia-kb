## extreme capsule ventral stream

**Kind:** atlas gap: no atlas label, findings unreachable  
**Areas:** `left_extreme_capsule` (7 / 4)  
**Suggested papers:** @Kummerer2013 [seed], @Oeckel2021 [seed]

7 findings, but neither JHU nor XTRACT labels the extreme capsule. Oeckel et al. 2021 and Kümmerer et al. 2013 measured it with tractography-derived templates from healthy subjects; extracting them documents the ventral-stream evidence, and their templates (if shared) would make the region reachable.

## thalamus basal ganglia

**Kind:** no evidence: measured but no findings  
**Areas:** `putamen` (3 / 1), `pallidum` (1 / 1)  
**Suggested papers:** @Hillis2002 [seed], @Sebastian2014 [seed], @Fritsch2022 [seed]

Lesions often reach the thalamus, caudate, putamen and pallidum (AAL measures all four), but the KB has no finding on the thalamus or caudate and 4 findings from one paper on putamen and pallidum (Radanovic & Mansur 2017 was extracted as a narrative review, without region-level findings). The subcortical-aphasia literature also shows that much of it reflects cortical hypoperfusion, which the report should be able to say.

## anterior thalamic radiation ifof semantics

**Kind:** thin evidence  
**Areas:** `ifof` (3 / 3), `left_inferior_fronto_occipital_fasciculus` (2 / 2)  
**Suggested papers:** @Han2013 [seed], @Li2017 [seed]

The inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus has 3 findings; the anterior thalamic radiation (measured by JHU) none. Both were linked to semantic deficits and verbal fluency in stroke cohorts that measured tract damage directly.

## frontal aslant sma fluency

**Kind:** thin evidence  
**Areas:** `supplementary_motor_area` (3 / 2), `left_frontal_aslant_tract` (1 / 1)  
**Suggested papers:** @Li2017 [seed]

3 findings on the supplementary motor area and 1 on the frontal aslant tract (reachable since the XTRACT atlas was added), which connects it to the inferior frontal gyrus and is linked to verbal fluency after stroke.

## insula apraxia of speech

**Kind:** contested in the literature  
**Areas:** `left_precentral_gyrus_insula` (7 / 5), `insula` (10 / 7), `frontal_operculum` (0 / 0)  
**Suggested papers:** @Baldo2011 [seed], @Hillis2004 [seed]

The insula's role in apraxia of speech rests mainly on Dronkers 1996 (precentral gyrus of the insula) and is disputed: Hillis et al. 2004 placed it in the posterior inferior frontal gyrus instead. The frontal operculum has no finding at all. Both sides should be in the KB.
